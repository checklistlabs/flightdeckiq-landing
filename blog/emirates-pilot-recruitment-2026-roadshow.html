Emirates Pilot Recruitment 2026: The Global Roadshow Starts This Week — Here's How to Get Ready

August 2026 4 min read FlightDeckIQ

If you've been waiting for a sign that it's time to pursue a Gulf flying career, this is it. Emirates kicks off its global pilot recruitment roadshow in Madrid on 6–7 August, the first stop in a campaign that will cross 14 countries in Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas over the coming months. The airline has publicly committed to hiring around 1,500 pilots across 2025 and 2026 as it prepares for a wave of new widebody deliveries — including the 65 Boeing 777X and 8 Airbus A350-900 aircraft ordered at the last Dubai Airshow.

And Emirates isn't alone. Etihad Airways has launched its own summer 2026 roadshow across Spain, Italy, the UK and Türkiye, while Riyadh Air continues international recruitment events as it takes roughly one new aircraft delivery per month. For experienced pilots, this is the most active Gulf hiring market in years.

What a recruitment roadshow actually is — and isn't

A roadshow event is not an interview. It's a chance to meet recruiters face to face, learn about the selection process, ask questions about life in Dubai or Abu Dhabi or Riyadh, and get on the airline's radar. Emirates has said its sessions are aimed at experienced commercial pilots, including candidates eligible for its Direct Entry Captain programme.

That distinction matters for how you prepare. You're not there to be assessed — but you are there to make an impression and gather intelligence you can't get from a website. Treat it accordingly.

Five things to do before you attend

1. Get your paperwork ready now, not later. Have your licence details, total hours, hours on type, and medical status at your fingertips. If a recruiter asks whether you meet the published minimums, "I think so" is the wrong answer. Check the airline's official careers page for current requirements before you go, and make sure your logbook totals are accurate and defensible.

2. Submit a clean online application first. For all three carriers, formal applications go through their official careers portals — a roadshow appearance doesn't replace that. A tight, error-free application with a professional CV is still the gate. If your CV hasn't been updated since your last type rating, fix that this week.

3. Prepare questions that show operational thinking. Anyone can ask about salary — the tax-free packages are well publicised. Fewer candidates ask about fleet transition timelines, basing, roster patterns, or command upgrade pathways. Thoughtful questions signal that you're evaluating the airline seriously, and the answers genuinely help you decide.

4. Start interview prep before you're invited. The biggest mistake candidates make is waiting for an assessment date before preparing. Gulf carrier selection processes are competitive and move quickly when hiring demand is high. Brush up your technical knowledge — performance, meteorology, air law, systems on your current type — and start practising structured answers to competency-based questions about leadership, decision-making, and CRM. If your sim currency on raw-data flying and non-precision approaches has faded, book practice time now.

5. Know the airline's story. Emirates added 2,000 pilots between 2022 and 2025 and currently has around 4,800 on its roster. Riyadh Air is building a fleet of 787-9s, A321neos and A350-1000s from scratch. Etihad is expanding again after years of consolidation. Recruiters notice candidates who understand where the airline is going and can articulate why they fit that trajectory — it's the difference between "I want a Gulf job" and "I want this job."

A note on sourcing Hiring figures and roadshow dates in this article are drawn from Emirates' own media centre and published aviation press coverage (linked below) — not from candidate reports. Roadshow schedules can shift, so check the airline's official careers page for the latest dates before you travel.

Why timing matters

Hiring windows in the Gulf open and close with fleet plans. When 1,500 seats need filling, standards don't drop — but the number of opportunities rises, and prepared candidates move through the pipeline faster. The pilots who convert this cycle into a job offer will be the ones who treated the roadshow announcement as the starting gun for serious preparation, not the finish line.

Whether your target is Emirates, Etihad or Riyadh Air, the fundamentals are the same: verified paperwork, sharp technical knowledge, structured competency answers, and a clear reason why you belong in that flight deck.

The roadshows are coming to a city near you. The question is whether you'll walk in ready.
