Etihad Airways is in the most aggressive hiring phase in its history. Under the Journey 2030 strategic plan, the airline is targeting 38 million passengers annually, a fleet of 200+ aircraft, and over 125 destinations worldwide. That means up to 2,000 new pilots by 2030 — and recruitment is already well underway. Etihad hired approximately 400 pilots in 2025 alone, ran international roadshows across Europe and South America, and relaunched its Cadet Pilot Programme for UAE nationals.

If you're reading this, you're probably considering an application — or you've already submitted one and you want to know exactly what lies ahead. This guide covers every stage of the Etihad pilot selection process as it stands in 2026: from the online application through to the simulator assessment in Abu Dhabi. No filler. Just the information you need to walk in prepared.

Whether you're a widebody Captain with 10,000 hours or a First Officer looking to make the move to the Gulf, the process is the same. Understanding what Etihad values — and how their selection differs from other Gulf carriers — is the first step towards a successful outcome.

Etihad Selection Process Overview

Etihad's pilot recruitment follows a structured, multi-stage pipeline. The airline recruits for Direct Entry Captains, Direct Entry First Officers, and — as of 2025 — cadets through their relaunched UAE national programme. Campaigns run year-round, with hiring volume tied to fleet delivery schedules under Journey 2030.

Here is the full selection sequence at a glance:

1

Online Application & Screening

Submit via the Etihad Careers portal. Licence, hours, type ratings, and recency are checked against minimum requirements for the role.

2

COMPASS & Psychometric Assessment

COMPASS computerised aptitude battery completed online before Abu Dhabi invitation, plus personality and behavioural assessments. Together these can take up to 6 hours.

3

Competency-Based Interview

Panel interview with training captains and HR. STAR-format questions covering communication, leadership, resilience, and CRM. 45-60 minutes.

4

Technical Interview

Aircraft-specific systems knowledge, ATPL-level questions, flight planning, meteorology, and performance calculations. Conducted by senior training captains.

5

Simulator Assessment

Full-flight simulator evaluation at the Etihad Training Centre in Abu Dhabi. B787 or A350 depending on role. Focus on CRM, workload management, and decision-making.

6

Medical & Document Verification

Aviation medical examination and thorough verification of all licences, certificates, and references. Conditional offer follows successful completion.

The entire process typically takes six to twelve weeks from initial application to conditional offer, depending on scheduling, batch sizes, and training slot availability. Etihad processes candidates in cohorts aligned with upcoming fleet deliveries and course start dates.

What Makes Etihad Different from Emirates

If you're considering both Gulf carriers — and most candidates are — you need to understand that Etihad and Emirates run fundamentally different operations despite being based 90 minutes apart on the same highway.

  • Abu Dhabi base, smaller operation: Etihad currently operates around 90 aircraft compared to Emirates' 260+. That means a tighter pilot community, more direct access to management, and a different day-to-day culture. Many pilots describe Etihad as feeling more like a family operation than a mega-carrier.
  • Personality and behavioural assessments: This is a significant differentiator. Etihad uses a comprehensive suite of personality and behavioural assessments alongside COMPASS (see our COMPASS preparation guide). Emirates relies more heavily on COMPASS alone. These assessments probe deeper into personality traits, derailers, and motivational drivers.
  • "Choose Well" brand values: Etihad's corporate identity centres on sustainability, innovation, and guest experience under its "Choose Well" positioning. Expect interviewers to test whether you understand and align with these values. This is not window dressing — it shapes how they assess cultural fit.
  • Training captains on selection panels: At Etihad, the pilots who will train you are directly involved in selecting you. This means the interview panel has operational credibility and will probe your flying experience with genuine understanding. You cannot bluff your way through technical or CRM questions.
  • More selective per capita: Etihad is growing rapidly under Journey 2030, but their smaller fleet size means each hiring cohort is relatively small. The acceptance rate per applicant may actually be lower than Emirates in some periods, despite Emirates having a larger absolute intake.
  • Fleet diversity: Etihad operates the Boeing 787 Dreamliner, Airbus A350, and Airbus A321LR — a more diverse narrowbody-widebody mix than Emirates' twin-type fleet of A380s and 777s. Your fleet preference and type experience may influence which role you're assessed for.

Neither airline is objectively better or worse. They test different things and value different qualities. The candidates who succeed at Etihad are those who prepare specifically for what Etihad looks for, rather than treating it as an Emirates backup.

Stage 1: Online Application & Screening

Everything starts at the Etihad Careers portal. You will create a profile, upload your CV, and fill out a detailed application form covering your licence type, total hours, type ratings, recency, and employment history.

Etihad's minimum requirements vary by role, but for Direct Entry positions you'll generally need an ICAO-compliant ATPL, a valid Class 1 medical, and minimum total flight hours that depend on whether you're applying as Captain or First Officer. Current type ratings on relevant aircraft (Boeing 787, Airbus A350, A320 family) are advantageous but not always mandatory.

Tip Your application form is the first document Etihad sees. Treat it like a professional submission. Ensure your hours are accurate to the logbook, your type ratings are listed correctly, and your employment dates match your CV. Discrepancies at this stage create doubt that follows you through the process.

After submission, Etihad's recruitment team screens applications against the published criteria. If you meet the minimums, you'll typically hear back within two to four weeks with an invitation to the psychometric assessment. During peak hiring periods — which 2026 certainly is — response times can be faster.

One thing to note: Etihad's roadshow events in Europe and South America sometimes offer fast-track screening. If you attend a recruitment event, you may complete initial screening on-site and move directly to the psychometric stage. Watch Etihad's LinkedIn and careers page for announcements.

Stage 2: COMPASS & Psychometric Assessment

The psychometric stage is where a significant percentage of candidates are filtered out. Etihad uses a two-part approach: the COMPASS computerised aptitude battery and personality and behavioural assessments. Together, these can take up to six hours. The COMPASS battery is completed online before your Abu Dhabi invitation; the personality and behavioural assessments may be completed remotely or in-person depending on the campaign.

The COMPASS battery

COMPASS is the same computerised psychometric system used across Gulf carriers. It measures four core cognitive domains that are directly relevant to pilot performance:

  • Spatial reasoning — mental rotation of objects, understanding three-dimensional relationships, interpreting instrument displays from different perspectives
  • Multi-tasking — tracking and responding to multiple simultaneous information streams, prioritising competing demands, and maintaining performance under cognitive load
  • Memory — short-term recall of sequences, patterns, and instructions; working memory capacity under time pressure
  • Information processing speed — how quickly and accurately you can absorb, interpret, and act on new information

The COMPASS battery typically takes 1.5 to 2 hours. It is strictly timed, and the difficulty adapts to your performance level. There is no pass/fail threshold published — Etihad uses your scores as part of a holistic assessment alongside the personality assessment results.

Personality and behavioural assessments

This is what sets Etihad apart from most other airline selection processes. The personality and behavioural assessment suite consists of three core areas:

  • Day-to-day personality traits — measures adjustment, ambition, sociability, interpersonal sensitivity, prudence, inquisitiveness, and learning approach
  • Stress-related derailers — identifies personality traits that emerge under stress: excitability, scepticism, cautiousness, boldness, colourfulness, and others. These are the traits that can undermine your performance when you're tired, pressured, or frustrated
  • Motivational drivers — maps your core motivational preferences: recognition, power, hedonism, altruism, affiliation, tradition, security, commerce, aesthetics, and science
Warning Do not try to "game" the personality assessments. They contain built-in validity scales that detect inconsistent or socially desirable responding. The best approach is to answer honestly while understanding what the assessments measure. Know your own personality profile before you sit the test — self-awareness is itself a trait these assessments capture.

The combined psychometric day — COMPASS plus personality assessments — gives Etihad a comprehensive picture of your cognitive abilities and personality profile before you ever sit in front of an interview panel. Your results inform the questions they ask you at the competency interview and the areas they probe more deeply.

Stage 3: The Competency-Based Interview

The competency-based interview is the centrepiece of Etihad's selection process. You will sit before a panel that typically includes one or two training captains and an HR representative. The session runs 45 to 60 minutes and is conducted entirely in STAR format — Situation, Task, Action, Result.

Etihad assesses six core competencies during the CBI:

  • Communication — can you convey complex information clearly, listen actively, and adapt your communication style to different audiences?
  • Leadership — do you take appropriate command when required, while also supporting others' leadership when the situation demands it?
  • Resilience — how do you handle setbacks, failed checks, career disruptions, or high-pressure operational situations?
  • Analytical thinking — can you break down complex problems, weigh options systematically, and make sound decisions under uncertainty?
  • Teamwork — do you operate effectively in a multi-cultural crew environment, share workload fairly, and contribute to a positive team dynamic?
  • Conscientiousness — are you thorough, reliable, and committed to standards? Do you follow procedures because you understand their purpose, not just because they're mandated?

The panel will ask you to provide specific examples from your flying career for each competency area. Expect questions like:

  • "Tell me about a time you had to resolve a conflict with a colleague in the cockpit."
  • "Describe a situation where you made a safety-first decision that was unpopular."
  • "Give me an example of working effectively in a cross-cultural team."
  • "Tell me about a time you identified and managed your own error."
  • "Describe how you've handled a period of high workload or operational pressure."
  • "Why Etihad? Why Abu Dhabi? What does your career look like in five years?"
Tip The training captains on the panel will follow up on every answer. They will ask "what happened next?" and "what would you do differently?" and "how did the other person respond?" Surface-level answers will not survive this probing. You need to know your STAR stories inside out — including the parts that didn't go perfectly.

Remember that your personality assessment results are available to the panel. If the assessments flagged a potential derailer — say, high cautiousness or low sociability — expect the panel to probe that area with targeted questions. This is not adversarial; it's Etihad verifying that you have self-awareness about your own tendencies.

CBI preparation checklist

  • Build 10-15 strong STAR examples covering all six competencies.
  • Practise answering on camera — record yourself and review the playback.
  • Prepare a compelling answer for "Why Etihad?" that references Journey 2030, fleet plans, and Abu Dhabi specifically.
  • Have examples ready for conflict resolution, error management, and cross-cultural teamwork.
  • Research Etihad's "Choose Well" values and be ready to connect your experience to them.

Stage 4: Technical Interview

The technical interview is conducted separately from the CBI, typically by a senior training captain or standards pilot. This is where Etihad assesses your raw aviation knowledge — and it goes deep.

The technical interview covers:

  • Aircraft systems — hydraulics, electrics, pneumatics, flight controls, fuel systems. Questions will be specific to your current type. If you fly the A320, expect A320 systems questions. If you fly the 737, expect 737 systems. Know your aircraft cold.
  • Flight planning — fuel calculations, alternate requirements, ETOPS considerations, weight and balance. Expect scenario-based questions rather than textbook recitation.
  • Meteorology — weather phenomena relevant to Gulf operations: sandstorms, wind shear, convective weather, fog patterns in the Arabian Gulf region. METAR and TAF interpretation.
  • Navigation — RNAV/RNP approaches, PBN requirements, holding procedures, SID/STAR construction. Understanding of ICAO PBN specifications.
  • Performance — take-off and landing performance calculations, contaminated runway considerations, engine-out procedures, obstacle clearance requirements.

The key thing to understand about Etihad's technical interview is that it is type-specific. The assessor will focus heavily on the aircraft you currently operate. They expect you to demonstrate the systems knowledge of someone who flies that aircraft every day — not someone who memorised a manual last week. If there are gaps in your knowledge of your own type, they will find them.

Good to know Etihad's technical interview is not designed to trick you. The training captains who conduct it are looking for solid foundational knowledge and — critically — the ability to explain complex systems in simple terms. If you don't know an answer, say so clearly rather than guessing. They respect honesty far more than bluffing.

ATPL-level theory is also fair game. Even if you passed your ATPL exams years ago, brush up on the fundamentals: principles of flight, instrumentation, radio navigation, and air law. The questions won't be exam-style multiple choice — they'll be open-ended discussions where the assessor wants to hear you think out loud.

Stage 5: Simulator Assessment

The simulator assessment takes place at the Etihad Training Centre in Abu Dhabi. You'll fly a full-flight simulator — typically a Boeing 787 or Airbus A350 depending on the role you're being assessed for. This is not a type check. Etihad is assessing your potential, not your current type proficiency.

Before entering the simulator, you'll receive a thorough briefing from the assessor covering the scenario, the departure and arrival procedures, weather conditions, and any specific instructions. Pay close attention to this briefing — it contains information you'll need throughout the session.

What the assessors evaluate

  • Basic handling and flight path management — smooth, accurate flying. Can you maintain parameters within commercial airline tolerances?
  • CRM and communication — are you briefing your colleague clearly? Sharing your intentions? Maintaining situational awareness as a crew, not just as an individual pilot?
  • Workload management — when multiple demands compete for your attention, can you prioritise effectively and delegate appropriately?
  • Decision-making — when the situation changes — engine failure, weather deterioration, system malfunction — do you make timely, sound decisions?
  • Response to abnormals — do you stay systematic when things go wrong? Follow the drills? Maintain the aviate-navigate-communicate hierarchy?

You'll likely fly with another candidate or an instructor in the other seat. Either way, communicate throughout. Verbalise your plan, share your decision-making, and work as a crew. The assessors are watching your crew dynamics just as closely as your stick-and-rudder skills.

Expect a mix of normal operations and abnormal scenarios. A typical session might include a standard departure, some upper airwork or holding, an instrument approach, an engine failure during a critical phase, and a go-around. The specifics vary, but the assessment philosophy is consistent: they want to see a safe, professional, communicative pilot who remains composed when the workload increases.

Tip Mistakes in the simulator are inevitable and expected. What matters is your recovery. Recognise the error, correct it, communicate it, and move on. The candidates who fail are not the ones who make mistakes — they're the ones who freeze, fixate, or try to hide the error. Demonstrate resilience and self-awareness under pressure.

How to Prepare — Practical Framework

Successful Etihad candidates don't rely on talent alone. They prepare systematically across every stage. Here is a practical framework that covers the full process:

COMPASS and psychometric preparation

  • Practise spatial reasoning, multi-tasking, and memory exercises daily for at least two weeks before your assessment date.
  • Use timed conditions — the real COMPASS battery is strictly timed and the pace catches unprepared candidates off guard.
  • Familiarise yourself with the personality assessment dimensions. Take a practice personality assessment and reflect honestly on your results.
  • Understand the concept of personality "derailers" — traits that emerge under stress. Know your own patterns so you can discuss them with self-awareness if asked.

CBI and motivation preparation

  • Build 10-15 STAR examples drawn from your actual flying career. Cover all six competency areas: communication, leadership, resilience, analytical thinking, teamwork, and conscientiousness.
  • Practise delivering your STAR stories on camera. Watch the playback. Identify filler words, vague language, and moments where you lose the thread.
  • Research Etihad thoroughly: Journey 2030 strategic plan, fleet composition, route network, "Choose Well" brand values, sustainability commitments, and recent news.
  • Research Abu Dhabi as a place to live. The lifestyle question is not casual — Etihad wants to know you've thought about relocation seriously.
  • Prepare a clear, concise answer for "Why Etihad specifically?" that goes beyond generic Gulf carrier benefits.

Technical and simulator preparation

  • Review your current aircraft systems in detail. If you haven't opened your systems manual recently, now is the time.
  • Brush up on ATPL theory fundamentals: principles of flight, meteorology, navigation, and performance.
  • Practise verbalising your technical knowledge — explaining systems to a colleague is different from knowing them silently.
  • If possible, get simulator time in the weeks before your assessment. Focus on CRM, communication, and workload management rather than raw handling skills.
  • Review standard callouts, briefing structures, and threat-and-error management frameworks.

Timeline: 4-6 weeks before assessment

  • Weeks 1-2: COMPASS practice, personality assessment research, ATPL theory revision
  • Weeks 2-3: STAR story development, Etihad company research, technical revision
  • Weeks 3-4: Mock CBI practice on camera, simulator time if available
  • Week 4+: Refine, rehearse, rest. Arrive in Abu Dhabi rested and ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the stages of the Etihad pilot interview process?

The process consists of six stages: online application and screening, COMPASS psychometric test and personality and behavioural assessments, competency-based interview with a panel of training captains and HR, technical interview conducted by senior training captains, simulator assessment at the Etihad Training Centre in Abu Dhabi, and finally medical examination and document verification. The full process typically takes six to twelve weeks.

What is the COMPASS test used by Etihad?

COMPASS is a computerised psychometric battery that measures spatial reasoning, multi-tasking ability, memory capacity, and information processing speed. It is used by several Gulf carriers and takes approximately 1.5 to 2 hours to complete. What makes Etihad's approach distinctive is that they use COMPASS alongside a comprehensive suite of personality and behavioural assessments, creating a more comprehensive psychometric profile than most other airlines.

How is Etihad's interview different from Emirates?

Etihad runs a smaller, more personalised process from Abu Dhabi. The key differences are the heavy emphasis on personality and behavioural assessments (which Emirates does not use to the same extent), the direct involvement of training captains on selection panels, and a higher weight placed on cultural fit with Etihad's "Choose Well" brand values. The overall process feels more intimate — smaller candidate groups, more individual attention, and deeper probing into personality and motivation.

What technical questions does Etihad ask pilots?

Etihad's technical interview covers aircraft systems, flight planning, meteorology, navigation, and performance. The critical distinction is that questions are type-specific — the assessor will focus on the aircraft you currently operate and expect deep, operational-level systems knowledge. ATPL theory fundamentals are also covered, with an emphasis on explaining concepts clearly rather than rote recitation. If you fly the A320, expect A320 systems. If you fly the 787, expect 787 systems.

How many pilots is Etihad hiring in 2026?

Etihad is in its most aggressive hiring phase ever. Under the Journey 2030 strategic plan, the airline targets a fleet of 200+ aircraft serving 125+ destinations, which requires up to 2,000 new pilots by 2030. They hired approximately 400 pilots in 2025 and continue aggressive recruitment throughout 2026, with international roadshows in Europe and South America. The relaunched Cadet Pilot Programme for UAE nationals adds an additional pipeline alongside direct entry recruitment.

"Etihad is building something different in Abu Dhabi — a premium airline with a smaller, tighter community than the mega-carriers. The pilots who succeed in their selection are the ones who've done the homework, know their own strengths and limitations, and genuinely want to be part of what Etihad is building under Journey 2030. Prepare thoroughly, be yourself, and let the process work."
