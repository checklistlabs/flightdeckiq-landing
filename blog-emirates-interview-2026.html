What to Expect in Your Emirates Pilot Interview 2026

Emirates is one of the most sought-after employers in commercial aviation. Competitive pay, a modern fleet, a tax-free base, and routes to every corner of the world make it a target for pilots at every stage of their career. It's also one of the most rigorous selection processes in the industry.

This guide breaks down exactly what the 2026 process looks like, what's changed since COVID, and what you need to prepare to give yourself the best possible chance.

Research basis This guide is compiled from candidate-reported experiences on PPRuNe (the Emirates interview thread has 279 pages and 4.4 million views), aviation prep sites, and cross-referenced reports from pilots who went through the process in 2023–2026.

The 2026 Selection Process — What's Changed

If you've read any older guides or prepped using pre-2022 material, be aware: the Emirates process has changed significantly.

The old process was a gruelling 4–5 day visit to Dubai involving group exercises, a psychologist interview, and multiple days of testing. That format is gone. The current process is leaner, faster, and front-loaded with online screening:

1

Online Pre-Screening

HireVue video interview (if English verification is required), followed by the COMPASS psychometric battery — abstract reasoning, numerical, spatial, and dual-task performance tests. A live digital panel interview (HR + Technical combined) rounds off Stage 1.

2

Dubai Assessment — Day 1

Welcome brief and facility tour. Advanced in-person COMPASS testing (significantly harder than the online version). Simulator brief, then simulator assessment on the B777 or A380.

3

Dubai Assessment — Day 2

Document verification and checks. Extended Emirates Medical. Accommodation and schools tour for candidates relocating with families.

Critical The group exercise and psychologist interview that featured in the old process have been removed. If a prep site still mentions these, the information is outdated. Focus your energy on COMPASS, the simulator, and your CBI answers.

Stage 1: Online Pre-Screening (HireVue + COMPASS)

HireVue Video Interview

Not all candidates receive a HireVue invite — it's used primarily for English language verification if Emirates can't confirm your proficiency from your application documents. If you receive one, treat it as a formal interview. You'll be asked standard introductory questions on camera. Dress professionally, ensure your background is clean, and speak clearly.

The COMPASS Test

The COMPASS psychometric battery is Emirates-specific and it is the biggest filter in the selection process. Most candidates who fail never reach Dubai. The test covers:

  • Abstract reasoning — complete the pattern in a 3×3 matrix. Speed and accuracy both matter.
  • Numerical reasoning — percentages, ratios, fractions under time pressure. Mental maths speed is essential.
  • Spatial reasoning — mental rotation of 3D objects. This catches a lot of people off-guard.
  • Dual-task performance — tracking a moving target while simultaneously responding to memory or attention tasks. This is a core pilot aptitude test and cannot be winged without practice.
  • Hand-eye coordination — joystick-style tracking task.
  • Memory sequences — recall strings of numbers, letters, or symbols.
Important — Format has changed in 2025 Multiple candidates who sat the COMPASS test in late 2024 and 2025 reported that the test format has been updated. Prep materials from LatestPilotJobs and older sites don't fully reflect the current version. Practise the underlying cognitive skills — don't rely on memorising old question formats.

Live Digital Panel Interview

This is a video call with an Emirates panel combining HR and technical questions. It replaced the in-person panel that used to happen on Day 3 in Dubai. Prepare for it exactly as you would a face-to-face interview — full prep, no notes visible, professional environment.

Stage 2: The Dubai Assessment

If you pass Stage 1, you'll be invited to Dubai for a 2-day assessment. Emirates covers flights and accommodation. The pace is tight — come rested, prepared, and with every document you've been asked to bring organised and easily accessible.

Day 1 starts with a welcome brief, then moves into the advanced in-person COMPASS testing. This is a more demanding version of the online battery. Candidates consistently report it's harder than expected even after passing the online version. Then comes the simulator brief, followed by the simulator assessment itself (see below).

Day 2 is largely administrative — document checks, the Emirates medical, and an orientation for those planning to relocate. The medical is thorough. If you have any known medical history that might be relevant, seek advice from an aviation medical examiner before your trip.

The Simulator Assessment

This is what most candidates fixate on, but it's widely misunderstood. The Emirates simulator assessment is not a skills test. It is a CRM and decision-making evaluation. Assessors are watching how you fly with another person, how you communicate, how you manage workload, and how you respond under pressure — not whether you grease every landing.

You'll be assessed on the B777 or A380 (depending on the role). Two candidates fly together, taking turns as PF. A sim brief is sent in advance — study it thoroughly. Typical profile elements include:

  • Basic handling — straight and level, turns, climbs, descents
  • ILS approach to minimums — full approach and landing
  • Engine failure after takeoff (EFATO) — memory items, aircraft control
  • Go-around from minimums
  • Non-precision approach (VOR or RNAV)
  • Rejected takeoff scenario
Tip — What assessors are actually looking for Cockpit discipline. SOP adherence. Clear, assertive communication with your co-candidate. Structured callouts. A calm, methodical response to abnormals. Being type-rated on the assessed aircraft is not mandatory — the profile is designed to be flyable without type experience.

HR & Competency Interview Questions

The live digital panel in Stage 1 covers both HR and technical questions. Emirates uses Competency-Based Interviewing (CBI) — every behavioural question requires a real example from your experience, structured using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

The most commonly reported questions across 2022–2026:

HRTell me about yourself. Walk me through your career to date.
HRWhy Emirates? Why do you want to work here specifically?
HRWhy should we hire you over another candidate?
HRWhy do you want to leave your current employer?
HRWhat do you know about Emirates? (Fleet, routes, strategy)
Competency — CRMTell me about a time you had a conflict with a crew member. What happened and how did you resolve it?
Competency — CRMHow would you handle a disagreement with your captain on a safety matter?
Competency — CRMTell me about a time you demonstrated good CRM skills.
Competency — PressureTell me about a time you were under significant time pressure or stress. How did you manage it?
Competency — ErrorTell me about a time you made a mistake or failed. What did you learn?
Competency — LeadershipTell me about a time you demonstrated leadership.
Competency — AdaptabilityTell me about a time you had to adapt to a new SOP or technology. How did you manage the transition?
The STAR trap most pilots fall into Emirates assessors are specifically trained to spot diffused responsibility — answers that say "we did" instead of "I did." Your answer needs to be about what you personally thought, decided, and acted on. "We" as a crew is fine for context. "We" as the subject of every action sentence is a red flag.

Technical Interview Questions

Technical questions are embedded in the live digital panel and may continue during the simulator brief and debrief. The depth expected depends on your current licence and type rating. Most reported questions are type-agnostic or focused on your current aircraft:

Type KnowledgeWhat are the key limitations of the aircraft type you currently fly? (V speeds, max altitude, temperature limits, max weights)
ProceduresWalk me through your engine failure after takeoff procedure.
IFRExplain the approach categories and minimums. What are the weather minimums for a CAT I ILS?
IFRWhat is the difference between a stabilised and unstabilised approach?
AirspaceWhat is RVSM and what are the requirements for flight in RVSM airspace?
MeteorologyWhat is a microburst? How would you handle a windshear encounter on approach?
Long Range OpsWhat is ETOPS and what are the key requirements?
CRM / TEMWhat does CRM stand for and why is it important? Explain Threat and Error Management.

Top 5 Tips From Candidates Who Passed

  1. 1. Treat COMPASS as your primary prep task More candidates are eliminated by COMPASS than by any other stage. Start practising abstract reasoning, dual-tasking, and spatial rotation weeks before your assessment — not the night before.
  2. 2. Have six STAR stories ready — and know them cold CRM conflict, error/mistake, time pressure, leadership, adaptability, and safety challenge. These cover 90% of the competency questions you'll face. Practice saying them out loud until they sound natural, not rehearsed.
  3. 3. Study the sim brief — not type-specific systems You'll receive the simulator profile in advance. Study the profile, not the entire B777 FCOM. Assessors know you're likely not type-rated; they're watching your approach to flying, not your type knowledge.
  4. 4. Know Emirates — really know it Fleet size, major hubs, current CEO (Sir Tim Clark), A380 operations, growth into new markets, cargo division. "I've always wanted to fly for Emirates" without substance behind it is a weak answer. Show genuine interest with specific knowledge.
  5. 5. The Dubai visit is still an assessment From the moment you arrive at the hotel to the moment you leave, you're being observed. How you interact with other candidates, how you behave in the van, how you conduct yourself at the evening briefing — it all matters. Emirates hires people they want on a 16-hour flight deck.
