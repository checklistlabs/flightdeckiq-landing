You've decided to apply to Etihad Airways. Maybe you've been watching their fleet grow. Maybe Abu Dhabi appeals to you more than Dubai. Maybe a friend who flies the Dreamliner told you the lifestyle is hard to beat.

Whatever brought you here, the question is the same: what does the Etihad pilot selection process actually look like in 2026, and how do you prepare for it?

This guide breaks down every stage — from the moment you hit "submit" on your application to the day you receive a conditional offer. No fluff. Just what you need to know.

Etihad's Selection Process Overview

Etihad's pilot recruitment follows a structured, multi-stage pipeline. The airline recruits for both Direct Entry Captains and Direct Entry First Officers, with campaigns running throughout the year depending on fleet demand.

Here is the full selection sequence at a glance:

1

Online Application & CV Screening

Submit your application through the Etihad careers portal. Your licence, hours, type ratings, and recency are checked against the minimum requirements for the role.

2

Online Psychometric & Aptitude Tests

If your application is shortlisted, you receive an email invitation to complete a battery of online psychometric and cognitive tests — typically within a set deadline.

3

Assessment Centre in Abu Dhabi

Successful candidates are invited to Abu Dhabi for an in-person assessment day covering the competency-based interview, group exercises, and simulator evaluation.

4

Simulator Assessment

A technical flying evaluation in a full-flight simulator at Etihad's training facility. Assessors evaluate your handling, decision-making, CRM, and workload management.

5

Medical & Document Verification

An aviation medical examination and thorough check of all licences, certificates, and references. Conditional offer follows successful completion.

The entire process can take anywhere from four to twelve weeks depending on scheduling and the volume of applicants. Etihad typically processes candidates in batches aligned with upcoming training slots.

Online Screening & Psychometrics

The online phase is your first real hurdle. After CV screening, Etihad sends a link to a psychometric and aptitude test battery. This is where a significant portion of applicants are filtered out — so take it seriously.

What the tests cover

Etihad uses a combination of cognitive ability tests and personality profiling. Expect assessments covering:

  • Numerical reasoning — interpreting data, charts, and performing quick calculations under time pressure
  • Verbal reasoning — reading comprehension, logical deduction from written passages
  • Spatial and mechanical reasoning — visualising rotations, understanding physical systems
  • Multi-tasking and attention management — tracking multiple information sources simultaneously
  • Personality and situational judgement — how you respond to workplace scenarios, crew dynamics, and authority gradients
Tip Don't treat the personality questionnaire as a box-ticking exercise. Etihad uses it to build a behavioural profile that carries into the interview stage. Inconsistent answers get flagged. Answer honestly, but understand what traits a professional pilot role demands — situational awareness, team orientation, resilience under pressure.

You'll typically have a fixed window (48–72 hours) to complete the tests once you start. Find a quiet room, use a stable internet connection, and do not rush. Many candidates underperform simply because they try to squeeze the tests in between other commitments.

Prepare before the link arrives

  • Practice numerical and verbal reasoning tests — the format matters as much as the content.
  • Work through multi-tasking aptitude modules in FlightDeckIQ to build speed and accuracy.
  • Time yourself. The real tests are strictly timed and the pace catches people off guard.
  • Get comfortable with a mouse and keyboard setup — touchpad users lose precious seconds.

The Assessment Centre in Abu Dhabi

If your psychometrics come back strong, you'll receive an invitation to Etihad's assessment centre at their headquarters in Abu Dhabi. This is the make-or-break stage — everything that follows depends on your performance here.

Etihad typically covers your hotel accommodation and provides transport between the hotel and the training facility. You'll be assessed alongside a small group of other candidates, usually between four and eight pilots.

What to expect on the day

The assessment day is long — plan for a full day starting early morning. You'll cycle through multiple stations:

  • Welcome briefing — overview of the day, introductions, logistics
  • Group exercise — a scenario-based discussion where assessors observe your communication, leadership balance, and ability to listen
  • Competency-based interview — one-on-one with a senior pilot assessor (covered in detail below)
  • Simulator assessment — technical flying evaluation (covered in detail below)
  • HR discussion — motivation, lifestyle expectations, contractual understanding

Dress professionally. Business suit, polished shoes, conservative grooming. First impressions carry weight — not because Etihad is superficial, but because presentation signals how seriously you take the process.

Good to know Etihad's assessment centre atmosphere is professional but not adversarial. The assessors want to see you at your best. They're looking for pilots who will represent the airline well — calm, competent, and genuinely interested in the role. If you've prepared, the day will feel structured rather than stressful.

Interview Format & Competency Focus

The competency-based interview (CBI) is the centrepiece of your assessment day. You'll sit across from a senior Etihad pilot — often a Training Captain or Check Captain — for approximately 45 to 60 minutes.

This is not a technical quiz. They already know you can fly. The CBI is designed to understand how you think, how you lead, and how you handle the moments that matter most.

Core competencies Etihad assesses

  • Decision-making under pressure — tell me about a time you had to make a critical decision with incomplete information
  • Crew resource management — describe a situation where you had to manage a conflict in the cockpit or coordinate with a difficult team member
  • Situational awareness — give an example of when you identified a developing threat before it became critical
  • Adaptability and resilience — how have you handled setbacks, failed checks, or unexpected changes in your career?
  • Leadership and followership — when did you step up to lead, and when did you deliberately support someone else's lead?
  • Safety culture — describe a time you raised a safety concern, even when it was uncomfortable to do so

Every answer should follow the STAR format — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Be specific. Use real examples from your flying career, not hypotheticals. The assessor will probe deeper on each answer, so know your stories inside and out.

Tip Prepare six to eight strong STAR stories that cover different competencies. The best candidates can flex a single story to answer multiple competency questions by shifting emphasis. Practice this out loud — recording yourself and playing it back is uncomfortable but effective.

Etihad places heavy emphasis on safety culture and CRM. They want pilots who will speak up, who will challenge authority when safety demands it, and who will admit mistakes rather than cover them. If you have a genuine example of raising a safety concern or managing a threat effectively, lead with it.

Simulator Assessment

The simulator check takes place at Etihad's training facility in Abu Dhabi. You'll fly a full-flight simulator — typically a Boeing 787 or Airbus A380 profile depending on the fleet you're being assessed for, though the aircraft type can vary.

An instructor will brief you thoroughly before you enter the sim. Pay attention to this briefing. Every detail matters — the departure procedure, the weather setup, the approach to expect, and any specific instructions about how they want you to fly.

What the assessors are watching

  • Basic handling and flight path management — can you fly accurately, smoothly, and consistently?
  • Standard operating procedures — do you follow SOPs, use checklists properly, and maintain a sterile cockpit when required?
  • Communication and CRM — are you briefing your colleague clearly, sharing workload, and maintaining awareness of the bigger picture?
  • Workload management — when multiple things demand your attention, can you prioritise effectively?
  • Response to abnormals — if a system fails or a situation degrades, do you stay systematic or do you chase the problem?
  • Judgment and decision-making — when it's time to go around, divert, or change the plan, do you make the right call?

You'll likely fly with another candidate acting as your co-pilot, or with an instructor in the other seat. Either way, communicate. Verbalise your intentions, share your plan, and work as a crew — not as a solo act.

Mistakes will happen. An imperfect approach, a late call, a momentary loss of situational awareness. The assessors know this. What they're watching is your recovery. Do you recognise the error, correct it, and move on? Or do you freeze, fixate, or try to hide it?

Medical & Documentation

After a successful assessment centre performance, Etihad will schedule your medical examination. This is conducted in Abu Dhabi by Etihad's approved aviation medical examiners.

The medical covers

  • Full physical examination
  • Blood work and urinalysis
  • ECG and cardiovascular screening
  • Audiometry (hearing test)
  • Ophthalmology examination — including colour vision and visual acuity
  • Drug and alcohol screening

If you hold a valid Class 1 medical from a recognised authority (EASA, FAA, GCAA), bring it along with all supporting documentation. Any pre-existing conditions should be disclosed upfront — Etihad's medical team would rather work with you on a declaration than discover something undisclosed later.

Document verification

Alongside the medical, Etihad verifies all of your professional documentation:

  • ATPL licence (original)
  • Type ratings and endorsements
  • Current Class 1 medical certificate
  • Logbook (original — with totals clearly summarised)
  • Passport (with sufficient validity)
  • Educational certificates
  • References from current and previous employers

Bring originals of everything. Copies are not sufficient for verification. Missing documents create delays and can hold up your offer while others in your batch move forward.

Etihad vs. Emirates — Key Differences in What They Look For

If you're considering both Gulf carriers, you need to understand that Etihad and Emirates — despite being based 90 minutes apart — run fundamentally different selection processes and value slightly different things.

  • Interview structure: Emirates uses an online panel interview before the Dubai assessment. Etihad brings you straight to Abu Dhabi for a combined assessment day after the psychometrics. The Etihad process feels more compressed.
  • Competency emphasis: Emirates places significant weight on the COMPASS aptitude test as a standalone filter. Etihad integrates aptitude testing earlier in the online phase and leans more heavily on the face-to-face competency interview during the assessment day.
  • Simulator format: Both airlines assess you in a full-flight sim, but Etihad tends to focus more on crew interaction and CRM during the sim check. The scenario complexity is comparable, but expect more emphasis on your communication and teamwork at Etihad.
  • Cultural fit: Etihad is a smaller operation than Emirates and tends to place more value on personality fit and long-term commitment. The HR discussion at Etihad often probes deeper into your motivation for choosing Abu Dhabi specifically and your understanding of the lifestyle.
  • Fleet and base: Emirates operates a single-hub, twin-type fleet (A380 and 777). Etihad operates a more diverse fleet including the 787 Dreamliner, A350, and A321LR, all from Abu Dhabi. Your fleet preference may factor into your decision.
  • Timeline: Etihad's process can move faster from assessment to offer, partly because of the smaller candidate volumes. Emirates batches tend to be larger with longer wait times between stages.

Neither process is harder or easier — they test different things in different ways. The candidates who succeed at both are the ones who adjust their preparation to match what each airline specifically values.

Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is the Etihad pilot interview?

Competitive but fair. The competency-based interview requires genuine preparation — you can't wing it with generic answers. The simulator is a standard airline assessment, not a check ride designed to fail you. Candidates who prepare their STAR stories, practise aptitude tests, and arrive rested consistently perform well. The biggest reason people fail is lack of preparation, not lack of ability.

Where is the Etihad assessment centre?

The assessment centre is at Etihad Airways' headquarters and training facilities in Abu Dhabi, UAE. Etihad typically arranges hotel accommodation and transport for candidates travelling internationally. The training campus is modern, well-equipped, and located near the airport.

What is Etihad's pilot salary?

Compensation varies by rank and fleet. First Officers typically earn between $8,000 and $12,000 USD per month tax-free. Captains range from $16,000 to over $22,000 USD per month depending on aircraft type and seniority. On top of base salary, the package includes housing allowance, annual leave travel, education allowance for dependents, medical insurance, and end-of-service gratuity. The tax-free element and allowances make the effective compensation significantly higher than headline figures suggest.
